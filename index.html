Arrive Paris Beauvais Airport
Visual guide — what to look for: Beauvais Airport is small and simple — one terminal building. You'll land on a small regional jet. Walk across the tarmac to the terminal (covered walkway in bad weather). Follow "Sortie / Exit" signs. Collect checked bag at baggage reclaim (one carousel). Paris Tips & Tricks

Metro Tips

  • Navigo Easy card: Load metro tickets, tap and go
  • Single ride: €2.55 | Day pass: €12.30 (5+ trips = worth it)
  • Metro runs 05:30-01:15 (Sun-Thu), 02:15 (Fri/Sat)
  • Keep your Navigo Easy card handy — have it out before reaching the turnstile. Don't fumble in your bag at the gate
  • Keep your ticket until exit — plain-clothes inspectors check frequently. Fine is €50+
  • Line 1 is your best friend: connects Champs-Élysées, Tuileries, Louvre, Le Marais
  • Line 4: Cité (flower market) to Montmartre/Anvers
  • If you miss a metro, the next one is 2-5 min away — don't stress!
  • Rush hour (08:00-09:30, 17:30-19:00): Metro gets very crowded. Keep bags in front. Avoid Line 1 and Line 13 if possible

Eating in Paris

  • Lunch: Formule/dish of the day €15-20pp (best value! Menu du jour is always cheaper)
  • Dinner: €25-40 per person at a bistro (more for tourist areas)
  • Breakfast: Croissant + café crème at bar = €3-5pp. Sitting on terrace = €6-10pp
  • Always say "une carafe d'eau" for free tap water (bottled water = €4-6 extra)
  • Bread is always free at the table
  • Service is included (15%) — tipping not required but round up
  • Avoid restaurants with picture menus near tourist spots — overpriced and mediocre
  • Budget reality: Two people eating 3 meals/day in Paris = €50-80/day total. Don't underestimate this!

Seeing the Eiffel Tower (from outside)

  • Best free views: Trocadero (across river), Champ de Mars gardens
  • July: sparkles every hour from ~22:00 to 01:00 (5 min each)
  • We're skipping the summit — Froggy has already been up, and the best views are OF the tower itself (Trocadéro, Champ de Mars, metro Line 6). More time for exploring!

Sun & Heat Tips (July = 27-29°C)

  • Sunscreen: Apply SPF 50+ every 2 hours — even in shade. You'll be walking outdoors most of the day
  • Hat/cap essential: Wide-brim or cap — you'll be exposed walking between sights. The Louvre glass pyramid area is full sun
  • Hydration: Carry a reusable water bottle. Paris has free Wallace fountains (green decorative fountains everywhere) and Wallace drinking fountains. Fill up often!
  • Peak sun: 12:00-15:00 — seek shade, sit at café terraces with awnings, or duck into museums/shops
  • Sun exposure warning: You'll walk 3-5km per day outdoors. With 27-29°C and strong July sun, sunburn and heat exhaustion are real risks — pace yourselves!
  • Froggy-friendly: Rest in shade every 45-60 min. Tuileries and Luxembourg Gardens have tree-lined paths and shaded benches

Late July weather: Typically warm and dry (25-30°C), long daylight hours, sunset ~21:45. Rain is uncommon but possible — check the forecast a few days before travelling. Pack light, breathable clothing. Sunscreen essential.

Safety & Pickpockets

  • Pickpockets are active at: Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Sacré-Cœur, Montmartre, metro (especially Lines 1, 4, 6, 13), Champs-Élysées, tourist restaurants
  • Peak times: 11:00-19:00, especially crowded metro carriages
  • Protect yourself: Use a cross-body bag worn in front. Zip all pockets. Never put phone/wallet in back pocket
  • Watch for: "Petition" scammers (girls with clipboards), ring trick (someone picks up a ring and asks if it's yours), fake police asking to see your wallet
  • At cafés: Don't leave phone/tablet on the table — snatch-and-run is common on terraces
  • At metro gates: People hovering near turnstiles may try to "help" you tap — they're distracting you while an accomplice pickpockets you
  • Emergency: 112 (EU-wide) or 17 (French police)

Restaurant Decision Flow — When the Menu Overwhelms

You're tired, hungry, and looking at a wall of French you half-understand. Here's what to do — in order.

Scenario What to do
Menu is all French and you're tired Close your eyes, pick one from each section (starter/main/dessert). Don't read every word. You'll eat well regardless.
You don't understand a dish Say: "Excusez-moi, qu'est-ce que c'est?" (Point at the item). They'll explain in simple French or English.
Froggy's knees hurt + need to sit NOW Any café with outdoor chairs. Sit first, look at menu second. Say "Juste un café" if you need a 15-min rest before deciding.
You're hangry and can't agree One of you picks the restaurant, the other picks the wine. Or: first person to say "I don't mind" loses the choice — the other decides.
Budget is tight but you're hungry Boulangerie sandwich + bench = €5-7. Crêperie = €8-12. Bouillon (fixed price menu) = €12-15. Skip sit-down if you'd rather spend on tomorrow's lunch.
Service is slow / they've forgotten you "S'il vous plaît?" with a smile and raised hand (not a snap). French servers expect you to politely signal them. Don't wait silently — they won't check on you.
You ordered wrong and don't like it Eat it anyway (it's probably still good). Or swap plates with each other. Don't send food back in France — it's considered rude unless truly inedible.

Golden rule: If you're too tired to decide, pick the plat du jour (dish of the day). It's always fresh, always what the chef cooks best, always good value. Zero decisions needed.
